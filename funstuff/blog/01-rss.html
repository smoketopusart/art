01: RSS

RSS Feeds have long since been a fascination of mine. I love the idea of being able to so easily keep track of every website I care about from one location, and the idea of using it to blog has always been tempting to me.

Unfortunately in 2026, a LOT of compatibility is dropped. The protocol will never fully die, thanks to podcasts using the format, but a lot of websites no longer support or correctly update their RSS feeds. Even sites that churn out feeds often don't work.

So, what's the benefit of them? In sites that support them, you can be told when stuff updates, and with what. It's basic, but it means, if you can implement it, you can have a feed of every social media you're on without having to check a million different websites!

Outside of social media, I've found it great for any sites that have updates that you want to pay attention to. Think webcomics, blogs, shops... I have a few RSS feeds dedicated to different shopping sites, so I know the exact moment a shop drop happens! Great if a place tends to sell out fast. Obviously, you can add any podcasts you care about, since those inherently are, well, RSS feeds. I don't use sites like spotify and stuff, so this is nice for keeping up with any podcasts I particularly care for! (That being said, I need to get into more podcasts) Many conventions also support RSS, which can be a good way to keep up with updates, especially for artists looking into artist alley!

In terms of writing an RSS feed, it essentially lets you share your thoughts and blog in a way that isn't dependent on any singular social media platform. It's all stored in one XML file, and people can access it in any form they choose!

Presumably, you could use it for news sites, but I fear this could get kind of depressing unless you can heavily curate it (Which, thankfully, the RSS feeds I've used all have ways you can filter out/remove entries you don't care about, so you could get a blacklist going for specific terms.)

There's a few tools I use for RSS feeds, but I don't consider myself some RSS expert, so I always recommend doing a little bit of your own research!

  • feedbro Is one I've been using as my in-browser reader. I don't really want a separate application for RSS feeds, so it's nice having just a button in-browser that shows any updates. It also has an option to check a page for feeds, but you have to doublecheck sometimes to make sure said feed actually has anything inside it and updates (Tumblr's built-in RSS feeds no longer seem to work, for example)

Sites with native RSS feed support include:

  • archive.org: Lets you keep track of specific tags/searches. There's one I use to find new art books added to a specific collection!
  • AO3: can get a feed showing different fics when you search for a fic, updates like once a day. great for hyper-specific ships and stuff!!
  • many torrenting sites have RSS functionality, which could be nice if you need to keep track of something that has sequential updates each week (not that I'd advocate piracy or anything lol, not providing links for this example)

Sites with support that feels a bit weak to me include:

  • Bluesky: You CAN get a feed from any bluesky account, but it doesn't show any images, and also opens in the default bluesky view (I use an external client for Reasons). Will show the basic post, but it's not ideal for keeping up with artists.

Sites with non-functional RSS feeds:

  • Tumblr: Sadly, every tumblr I've tried seems to not actually update when the blog updates, making the whole point of an RSS feed kind of useless... I assume this is a bug?
  • Instagram: Says there's a feed when I check with feedbro, but nothing is actually in the feed...
  • Twitter: Same issue as Instagram

 

Sites I'm currently testing to make sure they update:

  • Youtube: Can get a feed per different user
